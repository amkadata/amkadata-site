What data qualifies

The everyday records of how your team worked.

Buyers are most interested in real working history: how problems were discussed, planned, built and fixed. If your company used tools like these for a few years, there may be something worth looking at.

Code

GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket: commits, pull requests, reviews

Tickets

Jira, Linear, Asana: issues, sprints, project history

Docs

Notion, Confluence, Google Drive: specs, wikis, processes

Chat

Slack, Teams: working conversations between the team

Support

Zendesk, Intercom: customer issues and how they were solved

Sales

HubSpot, Salesforce: pipeline and account workflows

Product

Analytics events, logs, design files

Common questions

What founders usually ask.

What could my data be worth?

It depends on how much history there is, how complete it is, and what buyers want at the time. Published buyer ranges start in the tens of thousands of dollars. Some companies won't qualify, and I'll tell you honestly if yours is unlikely to.

Who are the buyers?

Companies that license business data to AI developers. I talk to several, so I can introduce you to whichever fits your data best. You'll know exactly who they are before you share anything.

What about personal data and confidentiality?

Buyers de-identify the data, removing names, emails and customer details, before it's used. You agree the terms of the licence, including what's excluded. If something is too sensitive to share, it stays out.

What does it cost me?

Nothing upfront. If a sale happens, any commission is agreed with you in writing before I make the introduction. If you decide not to go ahead, you owe nothing.

My UK company is being struck off. Is it too late?

Not if you move quickly. Once a company is dissolved, its assets pass to the Crown, so a sale needs to complete before then. Dissolution usually follows about two months after the Gazette notice. The earlier we talk, the more options you have.
